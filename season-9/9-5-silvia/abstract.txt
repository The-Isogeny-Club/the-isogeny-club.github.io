<p>In this talk, we will explore an algebraic approach to the Supersingular Isogeny Problem (SIP) by describing it through polynomial systems. After introducing the necessary background on Gröbner bases, we will present the standard modeling based on classical modular polynomials and construct two alternative models for chains of 2- and 3-isogenies using Renes' formulas. Our original motivation was to obtain complexity bounds for the SIP by studying the solving degree of these polynomial systems. Unfortunately, the usual tools turn out to be less cooperative than expected: we show that the systems do not satisfy the assumptions required to apply standard bounds on the solving degree. Along the way, however, we prove some interesting algebraic properties of these systems. Finally, we conclude with an experimental comparison between the modular polynomial and Renes' polynomial models. This is a joint work with Alessio Caminata and Andrea Sanguineti.</p>
